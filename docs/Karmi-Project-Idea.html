Karmi (कर्मी)

A compiler between approved architectural design and built reality — for Nepal's housing.

Team Amgain — Prabesh Amgain · Prabhat Amgain  |  Frogtoberfest 2026 (Leapfrog Technology)  |  Open source, MIT licence

The problem

In Nepal you cannot legally build a house without a Naksa Pass: drawings signed by a registered engineer, checked against the National Building Code, approved by your municipality. Then construction drifts. The window comes out 3 feet instead of 5. The stair is narrower. The setback shrinks. Nobody re-measures — and the homeowner cannot read the drawings they paid for.

Deviations from the approved design are how unsafe buildings get built — in one of the most seismically exposed countries on earth. The completion certificate (सम्पूर्ण प्रमाण पत्र) is supposed to require conformance, but there is no practical way to verify it.

The idea in one sentence

Upload your approved naksa and a few site photos — Karmi compares the two and hands you a plain-Nepali checklist of what to verify, with a 3D view that shows the approved house and the built one, differences highlighted.

How it works

  1. Approved drawing in. The naksa PDF is parsed into a machine-readable model: walls with thickness, doors and windows, room polygons, dimensions, orientation.
  2. Reality in. Site photos (or tape measurements entered on the phone) become an "as-built" model: construction stage, visible elements, approximate sizes — each with a confidence value.
  3. Compile the difference. A deterministic comparison engine matches every drawn element to the site evidence — respecting which wall it is on and where along it — and scores the difference by severity.
  4. Explain it. The output is a bilingual report: a numbered checklist in Nepali, an honest "what Karmi could not determine" section, and a 2D→3D view where the approved house builds itself and the as-built appears with differences in red.

What makes it different

Honest by design

Every number carries its confidence and its source. If the photos only cover one facade, the report says "could not be checked" — it never claims a wall is missing.

Never an accusation

Karmi has no word for "violation". Every finding is phrased as something to verify with your engineer or municipality — (कृपया जाँच गर्नुहोस्).

AI that does real work

AI extracts the drawing and the photos into structured data that code consumes. Delete the AI and the product stops — it is not a chatbot bolted onto a form.

Technology

  • Fully offline and free to run — no GPU, no API key, no internet needed for the core pipeline.
  • Open-weight AI only (Qwen, Llama, DeepSeek families, via Ollama or any open-weight endpoint) — closed models are refused in code.
  • Deterministic core: geometry, matching and severity are ordinary code; the AI never computes a number, and any AI-written narration is validated against the computed figures.
  • Vector-PDF parser (pymupdf), raster fallback (Otsu + run-length extraction), FastAPI web UI, three.js 2D→3D viewer, 77 automated tests.

Open-source contribution

Karmi's public good is bigger than the app: the first annotated dataset of Nepali naksa drawings paired with construction photos — consent-based (Privacy Act 2075), de-identified, licence-cleared (Copyright Act 2059) — plus the conformance engine and the visualisation, all MIT-licensed so others can build on them.
